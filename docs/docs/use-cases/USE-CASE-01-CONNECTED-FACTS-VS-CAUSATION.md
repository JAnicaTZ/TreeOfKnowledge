Use Case 01 — Connected Facts Do Not Prove Causation

A Four-Variable Propositional Experiment

«FACTS → PROPOSITIONS → FORMULA → TRUTH TABLE → LOGICAL OUTCOME

Not:

CONNECTED FACTS → AUTOMATIC CAUSATION OR GUILT»

---

1. Purpose

This experimental use case demonstrates how the Tree of Knowledge can separate:

- externally verified facts;
- propositional representations of those facts;
- a formally testable logical formula;
- the formula’s exact truth-table outcome;
- possible hypotheses; and
- conclusions that the available facts do not establish.

The example concerns publicly documented relationships involving:

- the Gates Foundation;
- the World Health Organization;
- the COVID-19 pandemic;
- remote work and schooling; and
- Microsoft Teams.

This is an educational reasoning experiment.

It is not an accusation, investigation finding or claim that any person or organization caused the pandemic or acted unlawfully.

---

2. Why This Example Is Interesting

A provocative shorthand might be written as:

Gates → WHO → pandemic → Teams → Gates

This looks like a closed circle. ⭕

However, the arrows conceal several very different types of relationships:

- institutional funding;
- historical company founding;
- public-health assessment;
- changes in working and schooling conditions;
- increased demand for collaboration software;
- increased use of a Microsoft product; and
- an alleged personal financial benefit.

A visible sequence of relationships is not automatically a valid causal chain.

The purpose of TREE is therefore not to confirm the apparent circle, but to ask:

«Can every arrow carry the logical meaning assigned to it?»

---

3. Verified Background Facts

3.1 Gates Foundation and WHO

WHO reports that philanthropic foundations provide a significant part of its funding and identifies the Gates Foundation as the predominant philanthropic foundation in that category.

Source:

"WHO — Current state of WHO financing" (https://www.who.int/about/funding/invest-in-who/investment-case-2.0/current-state)

This establishes a documented funding relationship.

It does not, by itself, establish control over WHO decisions.

---

3.2 WHO and the Pandemic Characterization

On 11 March 2020, WHO stated that COVID-19 could be characterized as a pandemic.

Sources:

"WHO Director-General’s opening remarks — 11 March 2020" (https://www.who.int/news-room/speeches/item/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-media-briefing-on-covid-19---11-march-2020)

"WHO COVID-19 timeline" (https://www.who.int/news/item/27-04-2020-who-timeline---covid-19)

WHO’s characterization did not cause the underlying outbreak.

It was an institutional assessment of an already developing international health emergency.

---

3.3 Pandemic Conditions and Collaboration Software

The pandemic produced a rapid transition toward remote work, remote schooling and online collaboration.

Microsoft reported that Microsoft Teams grew from approximately 44 million daily active users in March 2020 to 115 million daily active users in October 2020.

Sources:

"Microsoft Teams — 44 million daily users, March 2020" (https://news.microsoft.com/apac/2020/03/19/microsoft-celebrates-the-3rd-anniversary-of-teams-the-hub-for-teamwork-and-productivity/)

"Microsoft Teams reaches 115 million daily active users" (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2020/10/28/microsoft-teams-reaches-115-million-dau-plus-a-new-daily-collaboration-minutes-metric-for-microsoft-365/)

This establishes a substantial increase in Teams usage during pandemic-related remote work and learning.

It does not, by itself, establish why the pandemic occurred.

---

3.4 Bill Gates and Microsoft

Microsoft’s official history states that Bill Gates and Paul Allen started Microsoft.

Source:

"Microsoft is born" (https://news.microsoft.com/announcement/microsoft-is-born/)

This establishes a historical relationship between Bill Gates and Microsoft.

It does not establish that Bill Gates personally directed Microsoft Teams operations during the pandemic or that increased Teams usage produced a specific personal benefit for him.

---

4. Four Propositional Variables

The current Propositional Minimization calculator supports four variables.

These variables must represent complete truth-valued propositions—not merely people, institutions or events.

Therefore, we do not define them as:

A = Bill Gates
B = WHO
C = pandemic
D = Microsoft Teams

Names and objects alone are neither true nor false.

Instead, we define:

A = The Gates Foundation was a major WHO donor.

B = WHO characterized COVID-19 as a pandemic.

C = Pandemic conditions greatly increased demand for remote
    collaboration software.

D = Microsoft Teams usage increased substantially during
    pandemic-related remote work and learning.

Each variable now represents a proposition that can be evaluated as true or false using external evidence.

---

5. The First Propositional Test

The experimental formula is:

(A ∧ B ∧ C) → D

Meaning:

«If "A", "B" and "C" are all accepted as true, then "D" is expected to follow within this deliberately simplified model.»

The calculator accepts implication directly.

The same formula reduced to the project’s common logical basis:

{¬ (NOT), ∧ (AND), ∨ (OR)}

is:

¬(A ∧ B ∧ C) ∨ D

After applying De Morgan’s law:

¬A ∨ ¬B ∨ ¬C ∨ D

---

6. Exact Truth-Table Outcome

Four propositional variables produce:

2⁴ = 16

possible truth assignments.

The formula:

(A ∧ B ∧ C) → D

is false in exactly one assignment:

A = TRUE
B = TRUE
C = TRUE
D = FALSE

It is true in the remaining fifteen assignments.

Therefore:

TRUE assignments  = 15/16 = 93.75%
FALSE assignments =  1/16 =  6.25%

The formula is therefore:

CONTINGENT

It is neither:

a tautology

nor:

a contradiction

Expected reduced representation:

¬A ∨ ¬B ∨ ¬C ∨ D

---

7. Meaning of the 93.75% Result

The result may be stated precisely as:

«The formula is satisfied in 93.75% of all possible four-variable truth assignments, assuming that all assignments are counted equally.»

This is a logical truth-table coverage measure.

It is not:

- a 93.75% probability that every external proposition is factually correct;
- a 93.75% probability of a conflict of interest;
- a 93.75% probability that the pandemic was planned;
- a 93.75% probability that Bill Gates personally benefited;
- a measure of guilt; or
- a statistical or Bayesian conclusion about historical events.

The high percentage occurs because a material implication is true whenever at least one proposition in its antecedent is false.

The only falsifying assignment is:

A ∧ B ∧ C ∧ ¬D

---

8. What the Propositional Calculator Can Verify

The calculator can verify:

- whether the formula is syntactically accepted;
- how the formula is parsed;
- how its logical tree is constructed;
- how implication is reduced to "{¬, ∧, ∨}";
- how De Morgan’s laws are applied;
- whether the formula is a tautology, contradiction or contingency;
- the complete set of truth assignments;
- its DNF and CNF representations where supported;
- logically equivalent reduced representations; and
- the exact "15/16" logical outcome.

---

9. What the Calculator Cannot Verify

The calculator cannot independently determine:

- whether an external historical statement is true;
- whether a source is reliable;
- whether one institution controlled another;
- whether correlation represents causation;
- whether commercial benefit was intentional;
- whether Bill Gates personally obtained a relevant financial benefit;
- whether any conflict of interest existed;
- whether any person planned or caused the pandemic; or
- whether unlawful conduct occurred.

External facts must be established through reliable evidence before their corresponding propositional variables are assigned truth values.

---

10. The Apparent “Vicious Circle” ⭕

The compressed representation:

Gates → WHO → pandemic → Teams → Gates

is not yet a proven logical or causal circle.

A more transparent relationship map is:

Bill Gates
    → co-founded Microsoft
    → Microsoft provides Teams

Gates Foundation
    → provided substantial funding to WHO

WHO
    → characterized COVID-19 as a pandemic

Pandemic conditions
    → increased remote work and learning
    → increased demand for collaboration software
    → coincided with substantial growth in Teams usage

To close the final arrow:

Teams → Gates

additional evidence would be required regarding:

- relevant ownership during the examined period;
- measurable personal financial benefit;
- decision-making influence;
- intention;
- timing; and
- a legally or ethically relevant connection among those elements.

Without that evidence, the alleged circle remains a hypothesis—not a conclusion.

---

11. Hypotheses That May Be Asked

The documented relationships permit questions such as:

Did these relationships create overlapping incentives?

Did the funding relationship create a potential conflict of interest?

Did pandemic conditions create an extraordinary commercial advantage
for collaboration-software providers?

Did Microsoft Teams benefit from that market change?

Did Bill Gates personally receive a relevant and demonstrable benefit?

Does any available evidence establish planning, coordination or causation?

Asking these questions is legitimate.

Answering them requires evidence beyond the four-variable propositional formula.

---

12. What the Current Facts Do Not Establish

The currently listed facts do not establish:

Gates caused the pandemic.

They do not establish:

WHO caused the pandemic.

They do not establish:

The pandemic was created to increase Microsoft Teams usage.

They do not establish:

Bill Gates controlled WHO’s pandemic characterization.

They do not establish:

Increased Microsoft Teams usage proves personal wrongdoing.

These conclusions would require additional propositions, explicit inference rules and independently verified evidence.

---

13. Central TREE Lesson

TRUE NODES ≠ PROVEN CAUSAL ARROWS

Or:

VISIBLE RELATIONSHIPS ≠ CLOSED CAUSAL LOOP

In slightly less formal MacroHARD™ terminology:

«It LOOKS like a circle. ⭕
TREE asks whether every arrow can actually carry the logical weight. 🌳»

---

14. Why This Is a Suitable First Use Case

This example is intentionally small.

It uses exactly four propositional variables:

A, B, C, D

It demonstrates:

REDUCE → TRANSFORM RECURSIVELY → EVALUATE → ILLUMINATE

It also exposes an essential boundary of explainable symbolic reasoning:

«A reasoning engine may correctly process the propositions it receives while remaining unable to guarantee that those propositions are factually correct.»

That distinction is central to responsible Explainable AI.
